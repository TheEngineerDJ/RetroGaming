# CONTRIBUTION_TRUST.md

**Project:** RetroVault
**Role:** Community contribution and trust model
**Authority:** `Constitution.md`

## 1. Purpose

RetroVault should eventually accept community knowledge without allowing popularity to become truth.

## 2. Contribution types

Contributors may submit:
- facts
- evidence
- corrections
- photographs
- measurements
- compatibility reports
- identification suggestions
- historical information
- relationship proposals

Each contribution retains provenance.

## 3. Proposed vs canonical

User submission is not automatically canonical.

Flow:

`Submitted → Validated → Accepted / Rejected / Disputed`

High-risk changes may require multiple independent confirmations.

## 4. Reputation

Reputation measures reliability in specific contribution areas.

Do not create one universal reputation number that implies expertise in everything.

A contributor may be highly reliable for:
- Nintendo hardware

while having little evidence of expertise in:
- Sega PCB revisions

## 5. Evidence weighting

Contributor reputation may influence review priority and evidence weighting.

It must never override primary evidence.

## 6. Conflicts

Conflicting submissions remain visible to reviewers.

The system should explain:
- what differs
- who submitted each claim
- what evidence exists
- why one claim currently has stronger support

## 7. Moderation

Moderation protects data integrity, not consensus.

Moderators should not delete inconvenient claims merely because they are unpopular.

Fraud, spam, malicious metadata and intentionally misleading submissions may be removed, with moderation history retained where appropriate.

## 8. AI-assisted contributions

AI may help users draft, classify, normalize, or discover possible relationships.

AI-generated claims are proposals until supported by evidence.

Never mark a claim verified merely because an AI system produced it confidently.

## 9. Abuse resistance

Protect against:
- mass vandalism
- automated spam
- fabricated sales data
- fabricated benchmark results
- malicious filenames
- malicious links
- identity impersonation
- coordinated manipulation

## 10. Attribution

Contributors should receive attribution for meaningful contributions.

Attribution should survive later correction when historically relevant.

## 11. Reproducibility

A contributor submitting a measurement should provide enough methodology for another person to reproduce it where practical.

## 12. Guiding rule

**Community expands the evidence base. It does not replace evidence.**
